Twitch /twitch

Chat overlay, alert overlay with a FIFO queue, and chat-command handling. Alerts can fire any soundboard clip by name — set it in the Clip column of the Alerts table on the Twitch panel.

GET /twitch/api/statusconnection state (the primary channel's at the top level, every channel under channels), the accounts signed in, granted scopes, log
POST /twitch/api/config{"channels": [{"login": "name", "label": "beta", "enabled": true}], "overlay_channels": "primary|all"} — the channels to watch (first = primary, up to 5); {"channel": "name"} still sets the primary
POST /twitch/auth/logout{"login": "name"} signs that account out; no body signs out all
GET /twitch/api/queuethe alert queue (playing + waiting)
POST /twitch/api/queue/skip · clearskip the current alert / drop the whole queue
POST /twitch/api/test/chat{"text": "...", "channel": "name"} — fake a chat line (runs commands like !so as the broadcaster); channel is optional, the primary by default
POST /twitch/api/test/event{"kind": "follow", "channel": "name"} — fire a sample alert
GET · POST /twitch/api/fontsthe fonts uploaded for the overlays ({"fonts": [{"name", "file", "format", "url"}]}); POST a file (ttf, otf, woff, woff2) to add one; POST /twitch/api/fonts/delete with {"file": "..."} removes it
GET · POST /twitch/api/backgroundsthe picture library the overlays' backgrounds and alert pictures draw from (POST /twitch/api/backgrounds/delete to remove one)

Overlay looks

The Chat and Alerts tabs style the overlays: fonts (any Google font, or one you upload), pictures and 9-slice frames, per-alert accents and pictures, animations and your own CSS, with a live preview that is the real overlay page. All of it is stored under chat, events and alerts in config/twitch.json (POST /twitch/api/config takes the same shape).

Several channels

Add channels under Channels on the Connection tab (up to 5; the first is the primary). Each uses the account with its own name if you have signed in as it, else your default account, else anonymous chat. Every chat line and alert carries its channel. A source with no ?channel= in its URL shows what the panel's Chat and alert sources show says — the primary only, by default; …/twitch/chat?channel=name shows one channel and ?channel=all every channel (same for /twitch/events). Channels are independent: editing one, or signing an account in or out, reconnects only the channels it concerns, and a channel that cannot start keeps retrying without affecting the others; Reconnect reconnects all.

Feeding a bot

Set Forward URL on the Connection tab and every chat message and alert is POSTed there as JSON — {"type": "chat", "user": {...}, "flags": {"mod": true, ...}, "text": "...", "channel": "name"} for chat, {"type": "event", "kind": "raid", "channel": "name", ...} for alerts — channel says which watched channel it came from.

Shoutouts !so

You or a mod types !so channelname (or @channelname) in your own chat (with several channels: in the one you want it made in), and three things happen:

1 — official shoutoutthe real Twitch /shoutout banner (Twitch only accepts these while you're live)
2 — chat lineposted from your account; template configurable with {name}, {login}, {url}
3 — their clipstwo random clips from their channel play back to back on the Clips overlay — ephemeral, nothing queued or saved. Stop, or manually playing anything, cancels the rest

The chat-side parts need the moderator:manage:shoutouts and user:write:chat scopes — if you signed in before those existed, reconnect once in the Twitch panel. Without any sign-in the clips still play; only the chat messages are skipped (the Twitch panel log says exactly which piece did what).

Settings: config/twitch.json → "shoutout" — on, command (default !so), who (mods or broadcaster), native, message, clip, clip_count (1–5). Dry-run with !so somechannel in the Twitch panel's test-chat box, or call GET /clips/api/shoutout/{channel} directly.
