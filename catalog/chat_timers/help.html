Chat Timers /chat_timers

Messages Hexcast posts in your Twitch chat on a timer, as your account - your Discord link, schedule or socials. Each message has its own interval and waits for that many chat lines since it last posted (lines from Hexcast itself do not count), so a quiet chat is not filled with timers. Only while live (on by default) holds it while the stream is offline, and a stream starting restarts the clock: the first message comes one interval in. With several messages due, the one that waited longest goes first, and the next waits at least the seconds between two timer messages. {channel} and {uptime} are filled in.

It uses the shared Twitch connection: sign in there, with an account that may post in the channel (its own, or a moderator - the user:write:chat scope).

GET /chat_timers/api/statusevery timer with, per channel it posts in, what it waits for (interval · chat · live · gap · off, or "" when due), next_in seconds, lines_needed, sent, last_error; and problem when posting cannot work
GET · POST /chat_timers/api/config{"on": true, "gap": 60, "timers": [...]} — what you leave out stays; timers replaces the whole list
POST /chat_timers/api/timersadd one: {"text": "...", "every": 15, "lines": 5, "live_only": true, "channel": "primary|all|name", "name": "Discord", "on": true}
POST /chat_timers/api/timers/{id}change some of its settings (only the fields you send)
POST /chat_timers/api/timers/{id}/sendpost it now ({"channel": "name"} for one channel), due or not; its clock starts again
POST /chat_timers/api/timers/{id}/deleteremove it
WS /chat_timers/wslive {"type": "status"} updates, and every call above by name: {"cmd": "send", "id": "...", "rid": 1} (status, config, set_config, add, update, delete, send) — replies carry your rid
